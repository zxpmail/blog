# From "show me your code" to "show me your idea"

**Or: After the tyrant abdicates, what kills pretty talk**

*2026-07-13*

> One piece, two faces — with [Judging Fatigue](blog-essay-judging-fatigue.en.md). That face bites the emptiness and the person inside the judging machine; this face bites what remains in the hand after the tyrant abdicates: only talk.

---

Coding got faster. Satisfaction left.

Once: finish, run, watch it work — fullness was clear. Now: stare at what the model poured out, judge right or wrong, trust or not. End of day, hollowed. Not "still adapting." The work changed texture: from making toward judging.

Making: the hand leaves a mark, nothing becomes something, feedback immediate, yours. Tired body, fed mind.

Judging: assess what someone else — mostly AI — made. Spend judgment, produce nothing new, no refill. The more you judge, the emptier.

Before AI, making and judging mixed; the body of the work was making. After, the bulk of making was taken; what remains for people is mostly judging. Judging needs a person, for now. Judging does not feed. Not personal fragility — division of labor pulled the supply.

The core hides in the failure of an old line.

Talk is cheap, show me the code. For a long stretch, code could kill talk: compiler, tests, exit codes — fail means fail. Implementation and verification bound in one thing. One sentence fetched the idea and verified it.

Vibecoding cut the bind. Anyone can make a model pour code, neater than yours. Looking right, even running, is not the same as expressing your idea. Code sits there and no longer carries "this is yours." Judgment falls back: show me your idea.

Harder — not because idea suddenly got harder to think, because idea lost its referee.

You cannot run an idea. No exit code. Idea only appears as talk — spec, essay, comment, chat — and talk can be costumed: hollow can look deep. Worse: AI talks too, and often looks more like it has an idea. In the show-code age, code killed talk. In the show-idea age, code is cheap; nothing in hand kills talk. Real thought and fine disguise mix. The tyrant — that impartial compiler — abdicated.

Engineering offers a palliative: nail every claim back to something executable with an observable side effect. Runs: true. Fails: false. Misses pure tradeoffs, pure design. Where it reaches, unarbitrable talk becomes arbitrable code. Building the nail-gun is also judging, also drains. Judging against judging. Cruel. At least directed.

What then, standing in the middle. No cheap answer.

The emptiness is real. Don't prosecute yourself with "you should enjoy the efficiency" — that guilt steals what strength is left. Inspiration is not caught by gripping harder; grip is the judging posture. Loosen, and something might surface. Guiltily staring into space is not rest. And "catch that silhouette ahead" is not a stashable finish line — it only empties you further.

Writing this risks the same thing: packaging fatigue as depth with a framework — talk posing as idea. The self-reference does not lift.

No truth proclaimed. Only someone in the middle of the turn, translating "why am I so tired" into a shared condition. Saying it clear is the first step. If the next step is only saving this piece as "I get making vs judging," then returning to judge without making — the tyrant is still gone. In your hand, still only talk.
