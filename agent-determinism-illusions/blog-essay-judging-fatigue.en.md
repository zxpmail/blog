# Judging Fatigue

**Or: You built the judging machine — and forgot you still live in it**

*2026-07-13*

> One piece, two faces — with [From "show me your code" to "show me your idea"](blog-essay-show-idea.en.md). This face bites the emptiness and the person inside the judging machine; that face bites what remains in the hand after the tyrant abdicates: only talk.

---

End of day. Code ran. Comments answered. Verification scripts green. The person is empty.

Not tired. Tired knows how to sleep. Empty has nowhere to put itself. Once, finishing a stretch of code and watching it run left a mark in the hand. Now the mark is on the model's side. What remains here is judgment: trustworthy or not, pass or not, one more layer or not.

I asked a technical question: does this verification count as "AI judging AI"? Pull it apart — whether judge and judged are the same model; whether the verdict lands on text or on execution side effects — two things that should never have been mixed. My system dodges both. Technically, the question seemed settled. Fine.

Then the second question: so what is it for?

Harshly: much of it rediscovers what old programmers already knew — it lies, you can't trust everything, you have to run it yourself. Naive enough to sting. Writing articles is sometimes more comfortable than writing scripts — another way to dodge "bricklaying." The ledger still forced something real: one layer came from a gap a commenter nailed. Useful. Useful on the scarcity of others unwilling to spend the effort.

Safe zone ends here.

The third question breaks it: what am I actually doing all day?

When the answer arrived, I froze. All day: judging. Verifying, distilling, building process. Distilling this, distilling that. Not making. Judging what AI made. The difference looks small — and explains an emptiness I couldn't name. Reason knows useful. Feeling stays empty. How did I become this. No answer. Only more empty.

Later the emptiness found a structure.

Making tires the body and feeds the person. Judging spends judgment, produces nothing new, doesn't refill. AI took the bulk of making and left judging. Judging needs a person, for now. Judging does not feed. I was placed in the seat that is needed and does not nourish. Empty is not a mindset failure — division of labor pulled the supply line.

So the system that worked so hard to avoid self-evaluation is no longer only a design. It is judging-all-day engineered: build a machine made for judging, then go to work inside it. No wonder empty.

Colder still: judging itself got harder. Once, show me your code — compiler, tests, exit codes — could kill pretty talk. Now code is cheap, neat, even runnable, and no longer proves "this is yours." Judgment falls back to show me your idea. Idea has no exit code. Text can be costumed. AI writes text too, and often looks more like it has ideas. The tyrant abdicated.

I nail claims back to executable side effects — where that reaches, unarbitrable talk becomes arbitrable code. The cruelty: building the machine of resistance is also judging, also drains. Judging against the fatigue of judging. A loop. At least the drain has a direction.

In the middle of the turn, no cheap answer.

The emptiness is real. Not weakness. Not a duty to be grateful for efficiency. The guilt of "I should be content" steals strength by itself. Loosening the hand is how something might be caught; guiltily emptying the mind is still watching yourself with a judge's eye.

I hesitated a long time writing this. Framing emptiness as "making vs judging" may itself be text posing as thought. Opening a private confusion may be performance. Neither doubt lifts.

So no truth proclaimed. Only one person trying to translate "why am I so empty" from a private knot into a shared condition. Saying it clear is a beginning — even if the next second overturns it. Overturning means it still lives.

If you finish thinking "he named it," save, share, then go back to add another verification layer — the empty is still there. The machine is still there. The person is still inside the machine.
