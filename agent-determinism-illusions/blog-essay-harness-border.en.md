# The Boundary of the Harness

**Or: When three prompt lines replace two hundred, which lines did you delete**

*2026-07-13*

---

Models grow stronger. The harness looks lighter.

Once: two hundred lines for state, checks, blocks. Now: three lines in the system prompt, and the model "will handle it." The SQL guard becomes "please self-check before you output." The screen is clean. The green check remains. It feels like progress.

The danger is not that form got light. The danger is reading "looks light" as "is light."

Some write Agent = Model + Harness and ask whether harness is lasting DevOps or a patch for weak models. Others call everything between need and uncertainty a harness — prompts, scripts, permission filters, one pot. The word grows until it loses its edge.

The colder question is only one: among the two hundred lines swapped for a prompt, were a few never "reminders" but environment force — able to stop the model whether it wants to or not.

A code-level block stops. A prompt-level "please self-check" is a request. When model and verifier share a text channel, "I checked" and "I claim I checked" cannot be told apart. Force slides into conscience; the floor falls back to self-report — self-report wearing a green check. The wall the earlier pieces kept hitting: on a shared text channel, verification decays into compliance.

Will harness disappear? Off-scene, no answer. Exploratory writing can be thin; payments, SQL, risk controls must be thick. Don't ask whether to have one. Ask whether this layer is force or conscience.

It will not stay put either. Mechanical undergrowth will be eaten by the model — JSON, explicit state, repeated catches. New flesh grows at goals, resources, multi-agent order. Form moves; function remains: as long as real-world hands are still moved, probabilistic output must be translated into enforceable result.

The truly dangerous simplification is this: models are strong now, so the force layer can go.

If what you cut was a reminder, perhaps you truly saved. If what you cut was the net, what you gain is lightness — and a breach. The designer should not ask "can three lines replace two hundred," but "among the lines replaced, were some that must never be prompt-ified."

If you finish thinking "fair point," then move the gate into the prompt, delete the interceptor, watch the demo pass once — a light will catch. Hallway light. Not necessarily real.

Whether force remains is not how thin the docs look. It is whether, when the model lies, something can still hold it down.
